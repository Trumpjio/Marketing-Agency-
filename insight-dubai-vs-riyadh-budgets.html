Dubai vs Riyadh: where premium growth budgets are actually going.

Published October 2026  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  By OrcaPulse Editorial

Comparable spend, different mandates. A read on where regional CMOs are placing their 2026 bets.

Jan 2026 11 min read By OrcaPulse
Essay
⇌
Key takeaways
  • Dubai and Riyadh budgets are approaching parity at the top end — but the mandate behind the budget is dramatically different.
  • Dubai spend chases returns, speed, and defensible brand. Riyadh spend chases Vision 2030 alignment, local presence, and long-horizon positioning.
  • The media mix differs — Snapchat dominates in Riyadh, Instagram and Google in Dubai, with LinkedIn as a shared B2B spine.
  • Agencies that treat the two as a single "GCC" market lose both; the operating language of each is different.

The numbers everybody references and nobody separates.

The reported marketing spend numbers from GCC-wide reports blend Dubai and Riyadh into a single "GCC premium" category. Looked at closely, the two cities are converging on spend size and diverging on everything else — mandate, mix, measurement, timeline, buyer. Treating them as one market is a strategy error dressed as a geography shortcut.

At the top end of the premium bracket, 2024-25 budgets for a major brand are approaching parity between the two cities. A luxury hospitality launch, a sovereign-adjacent event series, a real-estate off-plan push, a government awareness campaign — each will carry comparable headline numbers in Riyadh and in Dubai in 2026.

The convergence of the headline numbers hides a divergence in the shape of the spend. The marketer who prepares one deck for both cities and swaps logos is the marketer whose Riyadh work underperforms by 30-50% without ever understanding why.

The mandate behind the spend — Dubai.

Dubai spend is overwhelmingly return-oriented. The buyer is a real-estate developer, a hospitality group, a global brand running a regional HQ, a fintech scaling into Asia and Europe, or a government entity acting with a corporate-style mandate. The measurement is comparatively quick — qualified visits, booked meetings, direct sales, loyalty-programme signups, pipeline conversion, RevPAR, SPAs, qualified MQLs — on 90-day and 180-day horizons.

The media mix is dominated by Google and Instagram, with LinkedIn as the B2B spine and TikTok, Snapchat, YouTube and Netflix MENA rotating in on specific audience targeting needs. English is the operating language of the brief, Arabic creative is produced for Gulf nationals as a parallel asset, and the agency relationship is typically short-cycle, performance-reviewed quarterly, and specialist-led.

The buyer in Dubai is comfortable with modern measurement, impatient with legacy frameworks, and willing to switch agencies on a 90-day underperformance window. This is a brutal, modern, premium market — and it rewards specialists who can prove outcomes with clean attribution.

Dubai spend chases return. Riyadh spend chases positioning inside a 20-year national narrative. A deck built for one will fail at the other.

The mandate behind the spend — Riyadh.

Riyadh spend is positioning-oriented and horizon-weighted. The buyer is a Vision 2030 PIF-backed entity, a sovereign fund affiliate, a government ministry, a national champion company, a family-office property vehicle, or an international brand establishing local presence as the entry fee to the next decade of market opportunity.

The measurement is longer-horizon — brand recall, share of voice, cultural alignment, native-market engagement, long-term relationship with government stakeholders — on 12-24-month windows. The media mix is dominated by Snapchat, YouTube and TikTok, with Instagram and Google filling specific gaps, and LinkedIn playing a role for B2B and institutional categories.

Arabic is the operating language of the brief, English is the parallel asset, and the agency relationship is longer-cycle, trust-based, and tied to local presence. Agencies with a Riyadh office and Saudi national senior staff win work that agencies with only a Dubai office cannot access, almost regardless of case-study quality.

The buyer in Riyadh is Vision 2030-anchored, comfortable with slower measurement, aligned with national-narrative framings, and reluctant to switch agencies without a serious reason. This is a different market with a different pace and a different buying culture.

The media mix — what each city actually buys.

Dubai mix (indicative): Google 25-30%, Instagram 25-30%, LinkedIn 10-15% (B2B categories), YouTube 10-15%, TikTok 5-10%, Snapchat 5-10%, Netflix MENA / OOH 5-10%, PR & events 5-10%.

Riyadh mix (indicative): Snapchat 20-30%, Google 15-20%, YouTube 15-20%, TikTok 10-15%, Instagram 10-15%, LinkedIn 5-10% (B2B/government), Twitter/X 5-10% (news-sensitive categories), OOH / Riyadh Season / event sponsorships 10-20%.

Snapchat alone can be the largest single channel in a Riyadh plan for a consumer category — a fact Western agencies routinely under-weight. Twitter/X in Saudi retains a cultural relevance that it lost in most other markets, especially for news-reactive and government-adjacent categories.

Mistakes that follow from blurring the two markets
  • One "GCC campaign" using Dubai-shot English creative with translated Arabic captions deployed identically in Riyadh.
  • Media mix duplication — a Riyadh plan that opens with Meta reach, as a Dubai plan would, under-weighting Snap and YouTube.
  • Measurement framework duplication — 90-day performance reporting applied to a Riyadh brand mandate that needs 18-month horizon measurement.
  • Agency staffing duplication — Dubai-based team pitching Riyadh work with no Saudi national seniority on the account.
  • Pricing duplication — Dubai-anchored retainers applied to Riyadh scopes without accounting for the longer sales cycle and relationship investment.

Where premium growth budgets are actually going in 2026.

Dubai in 2026 is still the primary spend city for consumer luxury, premium hospitality, real estate at the ultra-prime band, outbound-focused fintech, and global brands running regional HQs. The budget growth is modest and the margin pressure on agencies is real.

Riyadh in 2026 is where the new money is appearing — Vision 2030-anchored categories (giga-projects, cultural events, sports, hospitality, tourism), domestic consumer growth, Saudi-first B2B SaaS, and brands acquiring long-horizon local positioning. The budget growth is sharp and the agency opportunity is larger than the Dubai equivalent.

The useful move for serious agencies is a dual-office posture — a Dubai hub for operational excellence and global brand accounts, a Riyadh hub for Vision 2030 category authority and local presence. Agencies without a serious Riyadh presence will find themselves locked out of the fastest-growing part of the GCC premium market by 2028.

  1. Separate the two markets in your deck. One slide, two columns, side by side. Different mandates, different mix, different measurement. Make the difference visible to the client.
  2. Shift the Riyadh mix to Snap-first. With YouTube and TikTok as second and third. Google and Instagram are supporting, not leading.
  3. Lengthen the Riyadh measurement horizon. 12-24 months, brand-track with specific recall metrics, not just 90-day performance.
  4. Invest in Riyadh local staffing. Saudi national senior lead on every Riyadh account. Non-negotiable for serious work.
  5. Price the two cities differently. Riyadh retainers should reflect the longer sales cycle and the relationship investment. Dubai retainers can be sharper and more performance-tied.
