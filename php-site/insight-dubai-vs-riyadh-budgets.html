Dubai vs Riyadh: where premium growth budgets are actually going.

Comparable spend, different mandates. A read on where regional CMOs are placing their 2026 bets.

Jan 2026 11 min read By OrcaPulse
Essay
⇌

If you compare the top premium growth budgets in Dubai and Riyadh in 2026, the totals look remarkably similar. What they're being spent on is not.

Dubai: brand first, performance under it

The biggest Dubai budgets we see in 2026 are weighted toward brand, premium creative and content. CPMs are high. Buyers choose brands they recognise. Pure performance marketing burns cash fast here.

Where the money actually goes:

  • Instagram and YouTube production. High-end creative, often shot locally.
  • Content and SEO. Long-form, bilingual, built for both Google and AI search.
  • Performance media. Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok but weighted toward retargeting and consideration, not cold acquisition.
  • Experiential and PR. Events, launches, premium press.

Riyadh: platform-mix and procurement rigour

Riyadh budgets look similar in size but the platform mix is dramatically different. Snapchat and TikTok dominate consumer. LinkedIn is a bigger B2B channel than in Dubai. And procurement discipline is tougher.

Where the money actually goes:

  • Snapchat and TikTok. At campaign level, with dedicated creative not as afterthoughts.
  • Arabic content. Especially vernacular Najdi dialect for Riyadh audiences.
  • LinkedIn B2B. Vision 2030 has created a wave of senior Saudi executives who consume LinkedIn content.
  • Reporting and dashboards. Private firms demand data. The budget for measurement is bigger here than in Dubai.
A Dubai playbook will underperform in Riyadh by 20–40% on paid media, almost by default. The platforms are different, the dialect is different, and the procurement standard is tougher.

Where both cities are converging

Three places.

AI search. Both markets' affluent buyers are early adopters of Perplexity, ChatGPT Search and Google AI Overviews. AEO and GEO infrastructure is becoming table stakes.

Short-form video. Reels and Shorts dominate attention in both markets, even if the specific platform mix differs.

Vertical specialism. Both markets' top procurement teams increasingly prefer vertical specialists over generalists.

Where they're diverging

Three places.

Snapchat weight. Riyadh keeps it. Dubai dialled it down.

Arabic dialect depth. Riyadh demands Najdi; Dubai still works bilingually in modern-standard Arabic.

Procurement format. Riyadh is more formal more RFPs, more detailed scopes, more compliance. Dubai is more flexible more relationship-led, more conversational.

If you're splitting a 2026 budget

  • Treat Dubai and Riyadh as separate campaigns, not one pan-GCC plan with duplicated creative.
  • Expect your Riyadh creative to need Najdi versions, not just translations.
  • Allocate measurably more budget to Snapchat and TikTok on the Saudi side.
  • Build AI-search infrastructure for both.
  • In Dubai, over-invest in creative. In Riyadh, over-invest in reporting.
