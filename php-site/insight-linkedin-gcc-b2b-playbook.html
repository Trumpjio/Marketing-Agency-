Looking for a LinkedIn B2B marketing playbook for the GCC?

GCC enterprise buyers are on LinkedIn, but the ANZ and US playbooks do not travel. Here is what works for B2B marketing to the Gulf.

Book a strategy call
All insights

The ANZ and US LinkedIn playbook does not travel.

Western LinkedIn playbooks assume self-serve, product-led buying, and a cold-outreach-to-demo funnel. GCC enterprise buyers evaluate differently — longer cycles, more relationship-dependent, more conference-anchored, more trust-first. Running the Western playbook in Riyadh produces thin pipelines.

What GCC B2B buyers respond to on LinkedIn.

Founder and senior-leader content. Named case studies with recognisable GCC brands. Thought leadership from the technical team, not just marketing. Local reference customers posted about. Content that acknowledges regional regulatory context (DFSA, SAMA, QFC, CBK). Generic ANZ-written content underperforms because it reads as unfamiliar.

The campaign structures that work.

Account-based campaigns against a named list of 100-300 GCC enterprise accounts. Thought-leader ads from the senior team rather than brand-page posts. Document ads for whitepapers and reports. Lead-gen forms kept short — three fields max. Conversion ads sent to pages written in the buyer's language of trust, not generic SaaS landing pages.

Content that moves pipeline.

Industry reports with original GCC data. Named customer stories, including the local ones (Al-Rajhi, QNB, Mashreq, STC, Mobily — the kind of logos that unlock the next conversation). Market commentary from the senior team. Conference-tied content around GITEX, Money20/20 Riyadh, LEAP, Dubai Fintech Summit. These are what win attention and defend it.

The sales handoff most B2B programmes break.

A LinkedIn lead that arrives into a generic sales inbox dies. The win rate for GCC enterprise leads depends almost entirely on first-touch quality — named relationship manager, context-aware outreach, and ability to run the first conversation in whichever language the buyer prefers. The marketing programme that invests here outperforms the one that invests only in top-of-funnel volume.
