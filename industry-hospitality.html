Channels

Where to invest. Where not to.

Not every channel earns a place in a Hospitality plan. Here's where the budget belongs and where it burns.

Where to invest

  • Google Search + Hotel Ads
    Direct-booking driver. Position-ad competition with OTAs is brutal but essential.
  • Meta (Instagram + Facebook) paid
    Especially for F&B and leisure. Reels and location-tagged content drive discovery.
  • TripAdvisor + review platforms
    Trust layer. Response strategy matters as much as star rating.
  • Email + loyalty programs
    Where OTA margins get clawed back. Direct channel = direct revenue.
  • Snapchat and TikTok
    Younger local segments in UAE/KSA/Qatar for F&B and lifestyle experiences.
  • Owned content (blog, newsletter)
    Compounds over years. Where premium brand gets built.

Where not to

  • LinkedIn for leisure
    Wrong audience for most hospitality segments.
  • Pinterest for GCC hospitality
    Minimal regional traction compared to Instagram.
  • Programmatic without hyper-targeting
    Burns budget in a review-and-recommendation-driven category.
The campaign shape

What actually converts.

Offer, landing page and messaging the three things most Hospitality campaigns get wrong.

The offer

Hospitality offers work best when specific. 'Suite upgrade + breakfast + late checkout, direct booking only' outperforms 'luxury stays from X'. Loyalty perks drive repeat; limited-time windows drive first-time direct bookings.

The landing page

Property-specific or F&B-specific landing pages: hero visual (not just a hero shot but a sense of place), room/menu detail, pricing with direct-booking perk vs OTA, reviews, FAQ about check-in/dress code/COVID, booking widget visible always.

The follow-up

Booking windows vary business travellers decide in days, leisure in weeks. Email, remarketing and WhatsApp follow-up sequenced to that window. Loyalty members get different sequences than first-time guests.

The operating system

How we run a Hospitality program.

Three parts of the system: what we test first, what data we need, and what we measure.

What we test
  • Direct-booking perk variants upgrade vs breakfast vs flexibility.
  • Hero creative property shot vs experiential shot vs guest story.
  • Google Hotel Ads vs standard search ads.
  • Loyalty-upsell timing in post-booking sequence.
  • F&B retargeting from room guests (hotel restaurants) vs cold acquisition.
What we need
  • Historical booking data by channel + direct vs OTA breakdown.
  • PMS access (Opera, Mews, Cloudbeds, etc.).
  • Loyalty program membership data.
  • Email list and engagement history.
  • Review data and sentiment analysis.
What we measure
  • Direct-booking share of revenue (vs OTA).
  • Revenue per available room (RevPAR).
  • Average daily rate (ADR) and total revenue per guest.
  • Loyalty program growth rate.
  • Repeat guest rate.

Questions, answered

Common questions.

The real questions prospects ask us before signing an engagement.

Why does direct-booking matter so much?+

OTA commissions are 15–30% of revenue. Every booking shifted to direct is margin recovered. Over a year, direct-booking share lift is often the single biggest P&L mover for a property.

How do we compete with Booking.com on Google ads?+

Direct hotel ads integration + specific direct-only perks + brand-term defence. You won't win every click, but the right strategy claws back meaningful share.

Should we invest in TikTok for hospitality?+

Yes, especially for F&B and younger-skewing leisure experiences. Content-led, not ad-led, works best.

Is email still relevant for hotels?+

Yes and under-invested. A properly segmented list of past guests is the most profitable channel most properties have.

How do we handle negative reviews?+

Respond within 24 hours. Personally. Without defensiveness. The response is public it signals brand to future guests more than the review itself.

Can one agency handle a hotel + its restaurants?+

Yes if the strategy is integrated. The audience overlaps. The asset library overlaps. The loyalty data overlaps. Keeping them separate is a missed opportunity.
