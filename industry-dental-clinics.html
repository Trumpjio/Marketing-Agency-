Channels

Where to invest. Where not to.

Not every channel earns a place in a Dental & Aesthetic Clinics plan. Here's where the budget belongs and where it burns.

Where to invest

  • Google Search + Google My Business
    First stop for every patient. Local SEO is non-negotiable.
  • Instagram and TikTok
    Before/after content, patient stories, clinician reels.
  • Snapchat
    Strong in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait for younger aesthetic patients.
  • YouTube Shorts
    Procedure explainers, clinician authority content.
  • Meta paid lead ads with WhatsApp
    The strongest conversion path for most GCC clinics.
  • Review platforms (Google Reviews, Doctify, specialist platforms)
    Trust layer critical for high-ticket procedures.

Where not to

  • LinkedIn for consumer aesthetic
    Wrong audience. B2B channel, not patient channel.
  • Generic brand awareness ads
    Patients want proof, not atmosphere.
  • Programmatic banners
    Burn budget. Rare conversion in a trust-driven category.
  • Pinterest / Reddit
    Minimal patient discovery for most aesthetic categories in GCC.
The campaign shape

What actually converts.

Offer, landing page and messaging the three things most Dental & Aesthetic Clinics campaigns get wrong.

The offer

Clinic offers work best with specific outcomes and transparent pricing. 'Invisalign from AED X free initial consultation' outperforms 'premium orthodontic care'. Finance structures (monthly plans) are major conversion levers for aesthetic and ortho.

The landing page

Procedure-specific pages (not clinic homepage): clinician bio, before/after gallery, price range, finance options, booking form with WhatsApp, FAQs about safety and recovery. Trust signals above the fold.

The follow-up

Decisions take 2–8 weeks for high-ticket aesthetic. A follow-up cadence of personalised WhatsApp messages, educational content, and consultation reminders not just discount pings closes the gap.

The operating system

How we run a Dental & Aesthetic Clinics program.

Three parts of the system: what we test first, what data we need, and what we measure.

What we test
  • Procedure-specific landing pages vs clinic homepage.
  • WhatsApp-first lead capture vs form-fill.
  • Named-clinician creative vs generic clinic creative.
  • Before/after content vs lifestyle content vs educational content.
  • Finance-led vs premium-led positioning.
What we need
  • Procedure portfolio and pricing.
  • Historical lead data by procedure and source.
  • Clinician roster with licensing and specialities.
  • Patient CRM access.
  • Review and reputation data (Google Reviews, Doctify, Trustpilot).
What we measure
  • Qualified consultation bookings per channel.
  • Consultation-to-treatment conversion rate.
  • Average revenue per acquired patient.
  • Patient LTV by procedure type.
  • Review growth rate and sentiment.

Questions, answered

Common questions.

The real questions prospects ask us before signing an engagement.

What's a realistic monthly marketing budget for a clinic?+

In GCC, $8–40K per month depending on clinic scale and procedure mix. High-ticket aesthetic practices often spend more; family dental practices less.

How do we handle medical advertising rules in GCC?+

DHA, MOH and equivalents regulate what you can show, claim and promise. We work within the rules before-and-after use, claim language, and influencer disclosures all follow current guidelines.

Should we build an in-house content team or outsource?+

Hybrid works best. In-clinic team captures authentic clinician and patient content; we handle production, editing, strategy and distribution. Pure outsourcing feels fake; pure in-house usually can't scale.

What's the single biggest conversion lever?+

Response time + WhatsApp. Clinics that reply within 10 minutes convert leads at 2–3× the rate of 24-hour responders.

How important are Google Reviews?+

Core. Patients read 10+ reviews before booking a high-ticket consultation. A clinic with a 4.8 avg and 400 reviews outcompetes one with a 4.9 and 60 reviews.

Will dental tourism marketing work for us?+

If you have the capacity, pricing, concierge support and credentialing yes. If you don't, campaigns bring the wrong patients and brand damage follows.
